II. PARAMETER 1 — WHETHER THE DOCUMENTS ARE IN PROPER AMPLITUDE

  1. Calibration is in order. The record is acquired with a stated gain of 10 mm/mV and paper speed of 25 mm/sec, which are the universally accepted IEC/AHA standards. The display image reproduces a calibration block of 0.42 mV at the start of each row, consistent with the encoded gain. Each lead trace in the image scales correctly to one large square (5 mm) per millivolt.
  2. Digitisation headroom is comfortable. The 12-bit ADC over ±4 mV gives a least-significant bit of ≈ 1.953 µV — far below the noise floor of any clinical electrode interface, and well within the IEC 60601-2-25 requirement of ≤ 5 µV quantisation. No sample in any of the twelve leads approaches the ±4 mV rail. The maximum recorded excursions are V1 (−1.05 mV) and V5 (+1.04 mV), which is well within range.
  3. Peak-to-peak amplitudes are physiological. The peak-to-peak excursions are: I = 0.97 mV; II = 0.62 mV; III = 0.44 mV; aVR = 0.77 mV; aVL = 0.70 mV; aVF = 0.24 mV; V1 = 1.64 mV; V2 = 1.57 mV; V3 = 1.25 mV; V4 = 0.85 mV; V5 = 1.44 mV; V6 = 1.23 mV. These values are entirely within the expected physiological band and there is no evidence of low-voltage QRS (limb-lead QRS < 0.5 mV in all limb leads would have raised that concern; here lead aVF is modest at 0.24 mV but the precordial voltages are robust and the diagnosis is not low-voltage).
  4. No clipping, no saturation. A sample-by-sample search of all twelve leads for values exceeding ±3.9 mV returned a count of zero in every lead. There is therefore no amplitude clipping at the ADC ceiling and the morphology in V1, V2 and V5 — where excursions are largest by virtue of the underlying left bundle branch block pathology — is faithfully captured.
  5. Baseline wander is well-controlled. The median (DC) level of each lead lies within ±0.025 mV of zero in all twelve leads, confirming that the 0.05 Hz high-pass leg of the bandpass is functioning. Lead V3 (median +0.022 mV) shows the largest residual offset, which is clinically negligible.
  6. High-frequency noise is minimal. An RMS estimate of energy in the 50–150 Hz band on lead II returns approximately 3 µV — i.e. on the order of two ADC LSBs. The 50 Hz notch is clearly effective. The image shows mild, uniform 'fuzz' in V1–V3 corresponding to ordinary myographic activity; it does not obscure the QRS-T morphology.
  7. Opinion on Parameter 1: The amplitudes are proper. Calibration, gain, dynamic range, noise floor and baseline are all within accepted clinical standards. The record is fit for diagnostic interpretation on amplitude grounds.

This is the routine clinical presentation.
  2. Einthoven's Law is satisfied to machine precision. The first internal consistency test for lead labelling is whether II = I + III at every sample. Computed across all 7,006 samples, the mean absolute residual |II − (I + III)| is 0.00000 mV. This is mathematically perfect — the limb leads I, II and III are correctly named with the correct polarities. Any electrode swap (LA–RA, LA–LL, etc.) would produce a residual of the order of 0.5–1.0 mV, which is not present.
  3. Goldberger's augmented leads are correctly derived. Similarly, the mean absolute residuals |aVR − (−(I+II)/2)|, |aVL − (I−III)/2| and |aVF − (II+III)/2| are each ≈ 0.0005 mV — essentially limb-lead quantisation noise. The augmented leads aVR, aVL and aVF are therefore correctly named and correctly derived from the limb leads.

I is positive-dominant; aVR is negative-dominant (R-wave amplitude −0.555 mV against +0.211 mV) — exactly as expected when the electrodes are not reversed. V1 and V2 are negative-dominant (deep QS / rS pattern), V5 and V6 are positive-dominant (broad monophasic R), and the transition is in the V3–V4 zone. The lead names, channel polarities, calibration block, metadata bullet-points and clinical-finding text are all internally consistent between the JSON and the rendered image. There is no evidence of electrode reversal, of mis-named precordial channels, or of metadata-image mismatch.

V. PARAMETER 4 — WHETHER A DISEASE CAN BE DETECTED

  1. Underlying rhythm. The rhythm is regular sinus rhythm at 77 bpm. Upright P-waves are visible in leads I, II and aVF, with a constant P-R relationship; the RR variability across seventeen consecutive intervals is small (range 742–812 ms). PR interval is 164 ms — normal.
  2. Primary electrocardiographic diagnosis — Complete Left Bundle Branch Block. All three Strauss criteria for complete LBBB are satisfied on the record:
  3. QRS duration ≥ 120 ms — measured QRS is 148 ms (markedly prolonged);
  4. Broad, monophasic and notched R-waves in I, aVL, V5 and V6 — well-visualised in the display image, with the characteristic 'M-shape' of the lateral leads;
  5. Deep, wide QS/rS complexes in V1 and V2, with absent septal Q-waves in the lateral leads.
  6. Secondary repolarisation changes — appropriate. The ST-segment and T-wave changes seen in V1–V3 (concordant ST elevation with upright T) and in I, aVL, V5–V6 (discordant ST depression with T-wave inversion) are the expected appropriate discordant ST-T changes secondary to the bundle branch block, governed by Sgarbossa's principle of appropriate discordance. They do not, on their own, imply ischaemia.
  7. Axis. Mean frontal-plane QRS axis is −20° — left axis deviation, which is a frequent accompaniment of LBBB and may represent a degree of left anterior fascicular involvement.
  8. QTc. QTc by Bazett's formula is 456 ms — marginally prolonged for a male (upper limit ~ 440 ms). However, this measurement carries reduced reliability in the presence of LBBB because the wide QRS inflates QT. A more appropriate adjusted QT or use of the Bogossian/Rautaharju correction for LBBB would likely return the QTc to the normal range.
  9. Co-morbid risk profile evident on the record. The accompanying clinical data demonstrate Stage-2 hypertension (152/88 mmHg) and known Type-2 diabetes mellitus, both of which are recognised risk factors for, and frequent antecedents of, conduction-system disease in the sixth–seventh decade.
  10. Clinical significance and recommended next steps. Newly-detected complete LBBB in a 66-year-old hypertensive diabetic male warrants the following:
  11. comparison with any prior ECG, to determine whether the LBBB is new (mandates urgent evaluation for ACS) or chronic (less urgent but still significant);
  12. transthoracic echocardiography to assess left-ventricular size, systolic function (LVEF) and any regional wall-motion abnormality — LBBB by itself causes interventricular dyssynchrony and may reduce apparent LVEF;
  13. exclusion of myocardial ischaemia (high-sensitivity troponin, and where clinically warranted, coronary evaluation — bearing in mind that LBBB masks the ST-segment markers of ischaemia other than Sgarbossa criteria);
  14. optimisation of blood pressure (target 130/80 mmHg) and glycaemic control;
  15. long-term surveillance for progression to higher-grade AV block, and reassessment for cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT) eligibility should the LVEF subsequently fall to ≤ 35% with QRS ≥ 150 ms — a threshold this record's QRS already exceeds at 148 ms (and would meet at the ≥ 130 ms threshold under the current ESC 2021 HF guideline).
  16. Opinion on Parameter 4: Yes. A disease can be — and is — confidently detected on these documents. The record discloses complete Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) with left axis deviation and appropriate discordant secondary ST-T changes, occurring in a hypertensive diabetic male in his seventh decade. The label assigned by the device and confirmed on doctor review ('Left Bundle Branch Block — Doctor Reviewed') is, in my opinion, electrocardiographically correct.
